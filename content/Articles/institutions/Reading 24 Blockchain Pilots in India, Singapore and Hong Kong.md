I've added a Pilot Scorecard to [The Monsoon Ledger](https://themonsoonledger.com/). It tracks 24 projects in India, Singapore and Hong Kong that put money, securities or payments on a blockchain. Each project is checked against what the institution said it would do, using that institution's own sources. Read side by side, the pilots show how banks and central banks in Asia are using the technology, and it looks quite different from crypto.

## On a public chain, the issuer still picks the holders

![[pilots-rubric-heatmap.png|Rubric heatmap of all 24 pilots]]

Eight live pilots run on public networks such as Ethereum. **None of them lets an anonymous wallet hold the token.** Buyers go through whitelists or vetted distributors. The banks use the public network as shared infrastructure and keep the list of holders closed.

## Assets go on public chains, money stays on private ones

![[pilots-ledger-by-cash-leg.png|Pilots by ledger and cash leg]]

Tokenised funds, gold and structured notes from Hang Seng, OCBC, DBS and UBS sit on Ethereum, but the payment for them happens off the blockchain. Where the money itself moves on a ledger, as bank deposits or central bank money, the network is private or its operator hasn't described it.

The one live exception is [HKDAP](https://themonsoonledger.com/pilots/hk-anchorpoint-hkdap-stablecoin/), Anchorpoint's licensed Hong Kong dollar stablecoin on Ethereum.

## The token is often a copy

In **only 6 of the 24** pilots is the token clearly the legal record of ownership. For the Bosera, ChinaAMC, CSOP and Hang Seng fund tokens, the trustee still keeps the official register off-chain. Nine more issuers haven't said either way.

If you're assessing one of these products, ask which record wins when the token and the register disagree.

## Banks start with the safest assets

Five of the tokenised products are money market funds and three are gold. Both are priced daily and are easy to reconcile against a register that already exists.

## Central bank money is going on ledgers for banks, not for the public

Hong Kong [ended its retail e-HKD trials](https://themonsoonledger.com/pilots/hk-hkma-e-hkd-pilot-programme/) after they showed the public treated it much like a tokenised bank deposit. India's [retail e-rupee](https://themonsoonledger.com/pilots/in-rbi-e-rupee-retail/) has 1 crore users, yet **circulation fell from ₹1,016 crore to ₹772 crore** in the year to March 2026.

The wholesale side keeps adding uses. The RBI's wholesale e-rupee now settles government securities, certificates of deposit and SEBI's new [tokenised corporate bonds](https://themonsoonledger.com/pilots/in-sebi-demat-2-tokenised-corporate-bonds/). MAS has issued two [tokenised MAS Bills](https://themonsoonledger.com/pilots/sg-mas-wholesale-cbdc-interbank-lending/) settled in wholesale central bank money. The HKMA plans to settle interbank transfers in tokenised central bank money around the end of 2026.

## Few issuers publish volumes

Seven pilots have become ongoing services. HSBC, DBS and Partior run theirs without publishing transaction figures. Of the eight live public-chain pilots, only HKDAP has a published contract address, so for the rest nobody can check the issuer's account on the chain itself.

The clearest growth figure is Hong Kong's [tokenised government green bonds](https://themonsoonledger.com/pilots/hk-hksar-government-tokenised-green-bonds/): **HK\$800 million in 2023 and about HK\$20 billion in September 2026.** Even there, the HKMA hasn't said how much of that was settled in tokens.

## Shared bank ledgers still finish in the old system

This year DBS, OCBC and UOB, and separately HSBC and Standard Chartered, made their first live payments on Swift's blockchain ledger. The ledger matched and netted the payments, and final settlement went through existing systems.

## Each market chose its own route

India built state-run infrastructure: the RBI's wholesale e-rupee and the depositories' ledger for tokenised bonds, none of it on a public chain. Hong Kong licensed a stablecoin on Ethereum and lets funds issue token classes on public chains. In Singapore, DBS keeps its tokenised deposits on its own private chain and sells structured notes on Ethereum.
